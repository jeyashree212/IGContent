# Triangle development knowledge base

Southwest Wake County, built for content in her six markets: **Cary, Morrisville,
Raleigh, Holly Springs, Fuquay-Varina, Garner.**

**How to use this.** Every skill reads this file before writing a carousel,
story, or script. It exists so content can cite a real project by name instead
of saying "the area is growing."

**Confidence markers.** Every claim is tagged:

- `[V]` — verified against a source in August 2026, source listed at the end.
- `[G]` — general background, no source pulled. **Verify before it goes in a post.**

**Freshness.** Researched **August 2026**. Timelines slip and figures move.
Anything dated within the last twelve months should be re-checked before it goes
out under her license. Re-run this research every six months.

**Fair Housing still governs everything here.** These are facts about roads,
buildings, jobs and money. None of them may be turned into a claim about who a
place is *for*. Growth, infrastructure and amenities — never demographics.
Note especially that this file contains **no school-quality material**, by design.

**No town is the consolation prize.** Every market here gets its own strengths
told on their own terms. Cary is high quality, high cost and highly coveted —
especially West Cary — and she actively wants Cary clients, so Cary is never
the "before" in a before-and-after. Movement along the corridor is a different
stage or a different fit, never an escape or a downgrade. Beyond being wrong
commercially, comparative put-downs of whole areas edge toward steering.

## 1. The thirty-year arc

The shape of it, and the frame the rest of this file hangs on. `[G]` unless marked.

The Triangle spent the 1990s as three separate towns around a research park.
What changed the region was not one project but a repeated pattern: **road
capacity arrives, then jobs, then rooftops, then a downtown gets built to serve
the rooftops.** Cary ran that sequence first, Morrisville is running it right
now, and Holly Springs and Fuquay-Varina are somewhere in the middle of it.

Three structural drivers, in order of how much they moved the map:

1. **Highway capacity.** I-40, then the Triangle Expressway toll road (opened in
   stages from 2011), then Complete 540. Each one turned an hour-out town into a
   commutable one. This is the single most reliable predictor of where value
   moved next.
2. **Employment anchors.** RTP, then the biotech and pharma cluster that spread
   south and west out of it. Jobs at a specific wage level create demand at a
   specific price point — that link is the most useful thing in this file.
3. **Municipal downtown-building.** Towns that had no center deliberately built
   one. Cary did it and it worked. Morrisville broke ground in 2025.
   Fuquay-Varina is doing it with two downtowns at once.

**The pattern worth teaching followers:** the road comes first, and the
appreciation follows the road by several years. That is why what is *approved
but not built* matters more than what is standing today.

## 2. The last five years, by town

### Cary

- **Downtown Cary Park** opened 2023 — a signature civic park that reset the
  center of gravity for the whole town. `[V]`
- **Fenton**, 92 acres at the I-40 / Cary Towne Boulevard interchange in the
  Eastern Cary Gateway. Over 2.5M sq ft of retail, office, restaurant, hotel and
  multifamily at full buildout. Crawford Brothers Steakhouse opened late 2024; a
  hotel was announced recently. **Still no grocery store** — the district's most
  cited gap. `[V]`
- **Cary median home price ≈ $580,000** as of early 2026, up modestly on
  2025. `[V]`
- **Cary Towne Center — one site that stalled.** Not a mark against Cary, which kept growing straight through it. Epic Games bought the mall to
  build a headquarters targeted for 2024. The rezoning application was withdrawn
  for inactivity in December 2024, the site still sat vacant in April 2026, and
  Epic announced roughly 1,000 layoffs in April 2026. `[V]`

### Morrisville

- **Building a downtown from scratch.** ~25 acres around Town Hall Drive into a
  live/work/play center — a downtown the town has never had. Groundbreaking
  spring 2025, Phase 1A underway now: a four-storey apartment building, retail,
  and a community park with space for live music. Block A runs 24–30 months.
  Block C, developed alongside it, is the current Western Wake Farmers Market
  site. $250,000 in federal Community Project Funding. `[V]`
- This is the direct evidence for her line about Morrisville going from a
  pass-through to a destination. **It is happening right now**, which makes it a
  live story rather than a history lesson.

### Holly Springs — the biggest jobs story in her territory

- **FUJIFILM Diosynth Biotechnologies**: a $3.2B biomanufacturing site. Phase one
  began operating in 2025 with 8 × 20,000L bioreactors, adding finished-goods
  capability in 2026. A second $1.2B phase doubles it to 16 bioreactors. **+680
  jobs, reaching about 1,400 by 2031.** `[V]`
- **Amgen**: a second investment of **$1B and about 370 more jobs**, at an
  average wage around **$91,500**, phased across **2028–2032**. `[V]`
- Together these have made Holly Springs and southern Wake one of the country's
  biotech investment hot spots. `[V]`
- **Why it matters for housing:** ~$91,500 average wage is the number to reason
  from. That is a household forming, and it lands between 2028 and 2032 — which
  means the demand is *scheduled*, not speculative.

### Fuquay-Varina

- Population **more than doubled since 2010**, roughly **44,000** as of 2023
  Census estimates. Among the fastest-growing municipalities in the state. `[V]`
- **Two downtowns** — Fuquay and Varina — and a Downtown Growth Plan explicitly
  aimed at connecting them, plus live/work sites, festival space, parking,
  lighting and sidewalks. Land sale and incentives for a downtown project
  approved. `[V]`
- Growth drivers named by the town: **completion of the 540 outer loop** and the
  biotech and pharma expansion in **neighbouring Johnston County**. `[V]`

### Garner

- Thinner research than the others — **this is the weakest section in the file
  and worth a dedicated pass.** `[G]`
- Confirmed: $850,000 in federal funds for safety and capacity improvements on
  **Jones Sausage Road**. `[V]`
- Garner's structural win is Complete 540 Phase 1 terminating at the I-40 / I-42
  interchange in Garner. `[V]`

### Raleigh, and downtown in particular — her specialty

- **Complete 540 Phase 1 opened 25 September 2024**: 18 miles from the NC-55
  Bypass in Apex to I-40 / I-42 in Garner. Phase 2 extends another 10 miles to
  I-87 / US 64 / US 264 in Knightdale, **completing the outer loop, scheduled
  2028.** `[V]` This is the single largest structural change in her southern
  markets and it directly connects the 55 corridor.
- **Dix Park** — 308 acres, a multi-phase master plan begun in 2020 and expected
  to take 10–20 years. **Gipson Play Plaza (18 acres) opened 2025** with gardens,
  play structures, water features and an amphitheatre. Flowers Cottage reopens
  summer 2026 after a remodel; four buildings come down in 2026 to become
  parkland. `[V]`
- **Chavis–Dix Strollway** — see the correction below. `[V]`
- **New Bern Avenue BRT is under construction now.** Phase one awarded to
  Rifenburg NC for **$28.7M** (1 July 2025); phase two to Fred Smith Company for
  **$64.7M** (7 October). Second contractor began erosion control at Poole Road on
  22 June 2026, working toward WakeMed and the I-440 overpass. Most advanced of
  four planned Wake BRT corridors. `[V]`
- **Southern Corridor BRT** hit 30% design in autumn 2025 and is moving toward
  60%. Station renderings are public. `[V]`

## 3. Correction worth carrying

**On the Dix–Chavis connector.** Her instinct was right that this matters, but
the stage is earlier than "they are building it."

The project is the **Chavis–Dix Strollway**: a **1.3-mile** multi-modal cultural
trail linking John Chavis Memorial Park to Dorothea Dix Park, with **Lenoir
Street** the preferred route, connecting the Historic Fourth Ward, South Park and
Shaw University. Funded by **$1.5M** in federal Community Project money. Design
firm selection ran in early 2025, and the **concept plan is expected to complete
in August 2026** — design, bidding and construction all follow after that. `[V]`

So as of now it is **funded and in planning, not under construction.** Saying
"they're building it" would be wrong and a sharp local follower would catch it.
Saying "this is funded, the route is chosen, and construction has not started
yet" is both accurate and a better hook — it is genuinely early information.

### Market numbers, early 2026 `[V]`

- Wake County median: **$450,000**
- Raleigh citywide median: **≈$436,500**, up **2.4%** year over year
- Cary median: **≈$580,000**
- Active listings, Wake, January 2026: **3,528** — up **20.9%** on January 2025
- Supply: roughly **3.4 months**
- Homes still closing near **98% of asking**

**The honest read:** this is a market moving toward balance, not a buyer's
market. Inventory is up meaningfully and bidding wars have cooled, but prices
are still edging up and sellers are still getting nearly all of ask. Anyone
waiting for a real price drop is waiting for something the data does not
support. That framing — "more room to breathe, not bargains" — is both true and
more useful than what most agents are posting.

## 5. Content angles this unlocks

Ranked by how well they suit her, and how hard they'd be for a competitor to copy.

1. **"The road comes first."** Complete 540 opened in September 2024 and reset
   her entire southern market. Teach the pattern — capacity, then jobs, then
   rooftops, then a downtown — and she is explaining the Triangle rather than
   describing it. Pairs directly with the Highway 55 carousel.
2. **"$91,500 a year, arriving 2028."** Amgen's average wage and hiring window
   in Holly Springs. Almost nobody connects a specific wage to a specific price
   point on a specific timeline. This is her best convert-side angle.
3. **"They're building a downtown from scratch."** Morrisville, right now, on
   ground she grew up around. Her personal history makes this hers.
4. **"Funded, routed, not yet built."** The Chavis–Dix Strollway. Early
   information delivered accurately, for her downtown Raleigh specialty.
5. **"The mall that never became a headquarters."** Cary Towne Center. Post the
   thing that *didn't* happen — almost no agent does, and it buys more
   credibility than another good-news post.
6. **"Inventory is up 20.9% and prices still rose."** Correcting the "market is
   crashing" assumption with the actual number.
7. **"Watch the Southern Corridor."** BRT at 30% design. Where downtown Raleigh
   walkability goes next.

### Two rules when using any of this

- **Never turn a project into a promise.** "A BRT station is planned two blocks
  away" is a fact. "This will make your home worth more" is a prediction she
  should not make in a caption.
- **Date every claim.** Say "as of August 2026." Infrastructure timelines slip,
  and a dated claim ages honestly instead of becoming wrong.
