The short version

These are better than most of what circulates on YouTube. The frameworks are real — the competitor scoring math in particular is genuinely well thought out, and the carousel and story renderers are the right architecture for what you want.

But every one of them was written for the same fictional customer: an online coach selling a $8,000 program to strangers, on a Mac, paying for Metricool and a scraping API. You are a licensed agent in a relationship business, working under a brokerage, on a free budget. Four of the ten survive that translation intact. Two need their engine swapped. Four should not go anywhere near your account, and one of those has a specific reason I'll come back to.

VIRAL-REELS-PIPELINE.md Cut

This one I'd cut even if it were free, and the reason is in your own paperwork — see directly below.

The one real red flag

VIRAL-REELS-PIPELINE downloads another creator's reel with yt-dlp, rips their audio track, re-uploads that audio under your own video, and copies their on-screen text verbatim — the skill's own instruction is to approve the transcribed text "1:1."

Using trending audio inside Instagram is fine; Instagram licenses it. Extracting that audio to a file and republishing it as your video's soundtrack is a different act, and it routes around the licensing entirely.

From the eXp agreement in your AI Use Rules folder

The indemnification section lists, among the things you personally cover eXp for: "Agent's infringement of any intellectual property rights of any third party."

That moves this from an abstract copyright worry to a named liability you've already signed for. Not worth it for a reel.

Worth noting what that same clause means more broadly: the agreement binds you to eXp's separate Policies document, your state commission's rules, MLS rules, and the NAR Code of Ethics. The advertising rules that actually govern your posts live in those, not in the contract itself — which is why the sample ICA you uploaded doesn't contain them. That's the document to track down.

One piece of feedback, since you asked

The identity is genuinely good — warm, distinctive, and nothing like the grey-and-navy every other agent uses. Two gaps. There's no portrait lockup: everything you have is landscape or 4:5, and Instagram Stories are 9:16, so the mark and disclosure need a vertical arrangement. And blush behind black serif is lovely in print but sits at fairly low contrast on a phone in daylight — for carousel body text I'd hold the blush as the ground and darken the text toward near-black, or invert to brick-on-cream for slides that need to carry more copy.

Two honest caveats on the radar

  • Meta's free business_discovery endpoint returns a competitor's captions, likes, comments, and post timing — but not view counts on their reels. So the outlier math has to score on engagement rather than views. Same method, slightly noisier signal. It's the real cost of not paying for a scraper, and it's a fair trade at zero dollars.
  • The skill's thresholds are set for a huge niche — it only counts a reel as an outlier above 30,000 views. In Triangle-area real estate that would flag nothing, possibly for months. We'll set the floor from your competitors' actual medians after the first run, which the skill itself recommends doing.

Your list of eight is also under the ten-to-twenty the method wants, and they're all local agents. Adding three or four bigger national accounts would give the radar proven formats to pull from, not just neighbors.

Cross-posting to TikTok

Straightforward, with one design consequence worth deciding now. TikTok's posting API needs app review and isn't worth it for draft-only, so cross-posting means exporting the files and uploading by hand — a couple of minutes per post.

The consequence: Instagram carousels are 1080×1350 and TikTok photo posts want 9:16. Rather than cropping and losing text, the renderer should emit both ratios from one config — cheap to build in now, annoying to retrofit. Stories at 1080×1920 already match TikTok natively.

One caution: TikTok suppresses reach on clips carrying a visible Instagram watermark, so cross-posts should come from the rendered files, never from a saved-down Reel.

Guardrails I'll build in

Generic content skills have no idea you hold a license. These become hard rules in the config, not suggestions in a prompt.

  • Fair Housing. No caption may describe who a neighborhood or home is for — no "perfect for young families," no "safe area," no school-quality claims used as a proxy. This one deserves care given your bikeable-and-walkable angle, which lives close to that line and needs to stay on the amenities side of it: greenways, distances, transit, parks. Facts about the place, never inferences about the people.
  • Brokerage attribution on every generated asset, per your commission's advertising rules and eXp's Policies.
  • No claims about a property — price, square footage, status, days on market — unless it comes from a source you supplied. Nothing invented, nothing web-searched into a listing description.
  • No client detail without written permission, which rules out the proof-screenshot pattern the story skill leans on. Closed-transaction imagery only where you've cleared it.
